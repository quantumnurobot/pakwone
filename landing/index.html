BrainLock is building an open, auditable EEG platform for concealed-information research. It detects the P300 response a brain produces about 300 to 800 ms after seeing a detail it knows, and reports "can't tell" whenever the data can't support a conclusion. How it works

Three kinds of words, one question

The subject watches a fast series of words on a screen while a headset records their brainwaves. There are three kinds of stimulus. The test asks one thing: does the brain treat the probe like something it knows or like something it has never seen?

Probecrowbar

A true detail that only someone with inside knowledge would recognize. It must never have been made public.

Targetscrewdriver

A detail the subject is taught before the test. It gives a reliable "known" response to compare against.

Irrelevanthammer · wrench · pliers

Plausible but wrong details. They give the "unknown" response. There are always at least twice as many as probes.

  1. Lock the protocol

    Probes are vetted and hashed before any recording, so no one can change them after seeing results.

  2. Consent

    The subject gets a plain-language briefing and can stop at any time.

  3. Record

    About 180 trials at a 3-second pace, with live signal-quality checks.

  4. Analyze

    Two independent bootstrap tests. Both must agree before the platform reports "information present".

  5. Report

    A signed report with waveforms, confidence figures and trial counts, reproducible from the raw data.

Where we stand

The prototype already refuses to guess

Our open-source analysis pipeline runs today on simulated EEG. In 40 simulated sessions per group, it flagged no subject who lacked the knowledge. When the data was weak, it said so. Real-world accuracy must still be measured, and that measurement is what this campaign pays for.

What it does

Built for research, with the limits in plain view

  • Detects whether specific details produce a recognition response (P300)
  • Reports confidence, trial counts and rejected trials with every result
  • Treats "indeterminate" as a normal, visible outcome
  • Keeps neural data on the local machine by default, encrypted and deletable
  • Lets independent reviewers re-run any analysis from the raw recording
  • Does not detect lies, guilt, intent or general memories
  • Cannot screen people for unknown plans. Every test needs specific, non-public details
  • Is not validated for court or employment use, and we will not sell it for either until independent field studies support it
  • Cannot tell whether someone learned a detail innocently, from the news or during questioning

Risks and challenges

What could go wrong

The science is contested

Published P300 recognition tests report high lab accuracy, but independent replications and field results vary. Our validation study may show lower accuracy than hoped. We will publish the results either way.

Countermeasures

Studies show trained subjects can weaken P300 tests. We plan to measure this directly rather than claim immunity.

Hardware delivery

Headset rewards depend on supplier lead times and on passing our timing-accuracy checks. We will not ship a headset that fails them.

Regulation and IP

Neural-data privacy laws are changing quickly, and prior patents exist in this field. Legal review and a freedom-to-operate analysis are funded in the budget.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this an investment?

No. It is a rewards campaign. Backers get the rewards listed and nothing else: no shares, no tokens, no profit rights. If we ever offer equity, it will go through a registered crowdfunding portal with the required disclosures.

Is this the same as "Brain Fingerprinting"?

It uses the same general scientific method, the P300 concealed-information test, which is published in the research literature. BrainLock is an independent project with no affiliation with Brain Fingerprinting, LLC or Dr. Lawrence Farwell.

Can police or employers use it?

Not from us. The platform is for research and education until independent, peer-reviewed field studies show it is reliable enough for high-stakes use. Research licenses prohibit forensic and employment decisions.

What happens if the campaign falls short?

On an all-or-nothing platform, backers are not charged unless the goal is reached. If the campaign runs on a flexible-funding platform, we will say so before launch and publish a reduced-scope plan.

Is the software open source?

The analysis pipeline is open source today, so anyone can inspect how a result is reached. The examiner desktop app will be licensed to fund ongoing maintenance.
